Differences from CSPICE

The package keeps the names, the argument order, the units and the numerical results of CSPICE N0067. It departs from CSPICE where the C interface carries hidden state, output arguments or arguments for C memory, and in the argument order of a few calls; this page lists each departure.

The kind of an error is the short message of CSPICE in lower case where the same condition exists there, so SPICE(INVALIDDEGREE) becomes :invaliddegree.

SPICE.jl already returns its results, names a call that changes its argument with !, returns nothing for a failed lookup and counts from 1. For a program written with SPICE.jl, the rows that change are the static arrays, which cannot be changed in place where SPICE.jl returns a Vector or a Matrix; the error type, which is the SpiceError of this package with its field kind and not SPICE.SpiceError; the Window in the place of a cell; the context; and the arguments for C memory that SPICE.jl keeps, such as start and room of gdpool, nintvls of gfdist and maxn of bodvrd. A call that must return a value also throws where SPICE.jl returns nothing, as gdpool does for a variable that is not loaded.

No state between calls

CSPICE keeps buffers and saved search results between calls, for speed. This package keeps no such state: a query is a function of the loaded kernels and of its arguments. Three visible consequences follow.

  • Threads. Queries from many threads need no lock, and a kernel can be loaded while other threads query; see Contexts and threads.
  • Settings. The defaults of timdef, tsetyr and tparch, the tolerance of gfstol and the settings of dskstl belong to a context, not to the process. The tolerance of gfstol is the default of the keyword tol of each search, and a tol given to a search takes its place.
  • Repeatable answers. Where the answer of CSPICE depends on an earlier call (for example ckgp directly after ckgpav on overlapping segments, the dates in a text kernel loaded after certain str2et calls, or the four padding bytes of each segment summary of a binary PCK, which CSPICE fills from the last SPK or CK summary it handled), this package returns the answer that CSPICE gives in a new process.

Numerical results

Each routine follows the arithmetic of its CSPICE counterpart, operation by operation, including the order of the terms of a sum; fused multiply-add is not used. For the same kernels and the same Float64 input, a result is equal to that of CSPICE bit for bit or differs by rounding; Coverage and validation says how this was checked and gives measured differences.

Exact derivatives and the rates of CSPICE

Order 0 of spkder and of pxder is the result of spkpos and of pxform, and every higher order is the derivative of that result as a function of the epoch, with the velocity of the SPK data in the place of the derivative of its position. For an SPK type whose velocity is the derivative of its position, such as type 2 of the planetary ephemerides, the result is therefore the exact derivative. For a type that stores the velocity apart from the position, such as type 9, order 1 of a geometric position is the stored velocity, as in spkezr, and the partial of a dual-number epoch is the same velocity.

For a position, let s be -1 for reception and +1 for transmission. In the barycentric inertial frame, spkder differentiates

p(t) = r_targ(t + s ℓ(t)) - r_obs(t)
P(t) = R(t + s |pc(t)| / c) * A(p(t), s * v_obs(t))

where ℓ is the light time, A is the stellar aberration and R is the rotation from J2000 to the output frame. For the LT corrections ℓ(t) = |r_targ(t) - r_obs(t)| / c, the geometric light time, because the single iteration of CSPICE evaluates the target at that light time; for the CN corrections, which iterate the light-time equation until it converges, ℓ solves ℓ(t) = |p(t)| / c, and its derivatives satisfy that equation order by order. A is the identity without +S, and otherwise A(p, v) = p + |p| sinφ v̂⊥ + |p| (cosφ - 1) p̂, with v⊥ the part of v perpendicular to p and sinφ = |v⊥| / c. R is constant for an inertial frame; for another frame, the epoch of R is corrected for the light time to the center of the frame, which is the body that the frame is attached to (the Earth for IAU_EARTH), and pc is p with that center in the place of the target.

The velocity of spkezr differs from order 1 for two reasons. CSPICE takes the rate of the light time from the converged light-time equation also for the LT corrections, which gives a relative difference of about 1e-8 for the Moon seen from the Earth (8.4e-9 measured with de440s.bsp). CSPICE also takes the acceleration of the observer for the +S corrections from a difference of velocities over ±1 s, which gives a relative difference of about 1e-13.

For data that are polynomials in time (SPK types 2, 3, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 18, 20 and the binary PCK), the derivative of any order is the exact derivative of the polynomial. For the other types, and for frames that are defined by formulas, the package evaluates the algorithm on truncated Taylor series in time, which carry each derivative up to the requested order through every operation, and so obtains the derivatives of that algorithm without finite differences. In both cases a derivative has the smoothness of the data and no more: at the boundary between two records of an SPK, where a record is the stretch of time that one set of coefficients or states covers, the geometric position is continuous to rounding and the higher derivatives can jump. An aberration-corrected position also has small steps there, which Known limits of accuracy describes.

What is absent

Of the 651 routines that the header of CSPICE N0067 declares, 162 have no counterpart of the same name. Each of them is a facility that Julia provides, a routine that NAIF has declared deprecated and replaced, or a part of the C interface that the call conventions above make unnecessary. The toolkit programs (brief, chronos, mkspk, spkmerge and the others) are not part of the package. SPICEApplications.jl runs them from Julia through the NAIF executables. Coverage and validation lists the areas of the library that exist.
