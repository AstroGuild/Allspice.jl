Geometry

Sibling calls can throw different kinds for one condition, such as an unknown frame or radii that are not valid, because each kind is the one of the CSPICE routine that the call ports; see SpiceError.

A reception correction ("LT", "CN", with or without "+S") gives the target as the observer sees it at et, and a transmission correction ("XLT", "XCN") gives it where a signal that the observer sends at et reaches it; Aberration lists the corrections.

Points on a body

Allspice.subpnt — Function
subpnt([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr) -> (spoint, trgepc, srfvec)

Return the sub-observer point on target as seen by obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as subpnt_c of CSPICE.

  • spoint: the point, in km, in the body-fixed frame fixref of the target.
  • trgepc: the epoch at the point (seconds past J2000 TDB). With a light time correction it is et minus (reception) or plus (transmission) the light time between the observer and the point. Without one it is et.
  • srfvec: the vector from the observer to the point, in km, in fixref at trgepc.

method selects the definition of the point and the shape of the target:

methodPoint
"NEAR POINT/ELLIPSOID"point of the reference ellipsoid nearest to the observer
"INTERCEPT/ELLIPSOID"where the line from the observer to the target center meets the ellipsoid
"NADIR/DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"where the ray from the observer toward the nearest point of the ellipsoid meets the DSK surface
"INTERCEPT/DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"where the ray from the observer toward the target center meets the DSK surface

Case is not significant. UNPRIORITIZED means that all loaded DSK segments of the target are used, whatever their load order. A DSK method can end with "/SURFACES = <list>", a list of surface names or IDs. The older forms "Near point: ellipsoid" and "Intercept: ellipsoid" are also valid.

abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. Bodies and frames are names or integer IDs. With an ellipsoid method, et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual, and the call does not allocate when the bodies and the frame are integer IDs. A DSK method uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body;
  • :noframe if fixref is not known, :invalidframe if it is not centered on target;
  • :badmethodsyntax if method is malformed, :badpriorityspec if a DSK method does not have UNPRIORITIZED, :invalidsubtype if it has no valid point definition;
  • :noseparation if the observer is at the target center;
  • :subpointnotfound if the ray misses the DSK surface, :degeneratecase if it misses the ellipsoid;
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of target are missing or not valid, :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded and :dskdatanotfound if no DSK data are loaded for it (DSK method).

Also throws the errors of spkpos.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of Mars, and a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
spoint, trgepc, srfvec = subpnt("NEAR POINT/ELLIPSOID", "MARS", et, "IAU_MARS", "LT+S", "EARTH")
radius, lon, lat = reclat(spoint)      # km, rad, rad (planetocentric)
distance = vnorm(srfvec)               # km from the Earth to the point

See also subslr, sincpt, gfsubc.

Allspice.subslr — Function
subslr([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr) -> (spoint, trgepc, srfvec)

Return the sub-solar point on target as seen by obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as subslr_c of CSPICE.

  • spoint: the point, in km, in the body-fixed frame fixref of the target.
  • trgepc: the epoch at the point (seconds past J2000 TDB). With a light time correction it is et minus the light time between the observer and the point. Without one it is et.
  • srfvec: the vector from the observer to the point, in km, in fixref at trgepc.

The methods are those of subpnt, with the Sun in place of the observer in the definition of the point. With a correction, the Sun is taken where it appears from the point at trgepc. The other arguments and the notes on et, allocation and the dskstl tolerances are those of subpnt.

Throws SpiceError(:notsupported) if abcorr is a transmission correction, and SpiceError(:invalidtarget) if target is the Sun. The other kinds are those of subpnt, except :bodiesnotdistinct and :noseparation: the observer and the target can be the same body.

See also subpnt, ilumin.

Allspice.sincpt — Function
sincpt([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr, dref, dvec)
    -> (spoint, trgepc, srfvec) or nothing

Return the first point where the ray from obsrvr along dvec meets the surface of target, as (spoint, trgepc, srfvec), or nothing if the ray misses the target. As sincpt_c of CSPICE, which returns a found flag instead of nothing.

  • spoint: the intercept, in km, in the body-fixed frame fixref of the target.
  • trgepc: the epoch at the intercept (seconds past J2000 TDB). With a light time correction it is et minus (reception) or plus (transmission) the light time between the observer and the intercept. Without one it is et.
  • srfvec: the vector from the observer to the intercept, in km, in fixref at trgepc.

dvec is the ray direction (3 elements, not zero) in the frame dref. A dref that is inertial or centered on the observer is evaluated at et. With a light time correction, any other dref is evaluated at et corrected for the light time to its center. With a +S correction the direction is corrected for stellar aberration.

method is "ELLIPSOID" or "DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"; the DSK form can end with "/SURFACES = <list>". Case is not significant. abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. et is in seconds past J2000 TDB. With "ELLIPSOID", et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual, and the call does not allocate when the bodies and the frames are integer IDs. A DSK method uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body;
  • :noframe if fixref or dref is not known, :invalidframe if fixref is not centered on target;
  • :zerovector if dvec is zero, :badarg if it does not have 3 elements;
  • :badmethodsyntax if method is malformed, :badpriorityspec if a DSK method does not have UNPRIORITIZED, :invalidmethod if it holds a sub-point type such as NEAR POINT;
  • :noseparation if the observer is at the target center;
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of target are missing or not valid, :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded and :dskdatanotfound if no DSK data are loaded for it (DSK method).

Also throws the errors of spkpos.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of Mars, and a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
toward_mars, _ = spkpos("MARS", et, "J2000", "LT+S", "EARTH")
hit = sincpt("ELLIPSOID", "MARS", et, "IAU_MARS", "LT+S", "EARTH", "J2000", toward_mars)
if hit !== nothing
    spoint, trgepc, srfvec = hit
    radius, lon, lat = reclat(spoint)  # km, rad, rad
end

See also subpnt, dskxsi, gfsntc, fovray.

Allspice.srfrec — Function
srfrec([ctx,] body, lon, lat) -> SVector{3}

Return the point of the reference ellipsoid of body at the planetocentric longitude lon and latitude lat (radians), in km in the body-fixed frame, as srfrec_c of CSPICE.

body is a name or an integer ID. The radii come from the pool variable BODY<id>_RADII. The call does not allocate when body is an integer ID in 1:999.

Throws SpiceError(:idcodenotfound) if the body name is not known. Throws SpiceError(:kernelvarnotfound) if the radii are not loaded, SpiceError(:invalidcount) if there are not 3 of them, and SpiceError(:badaxislength) if one is not positive.

See also latsrf, latrec, bodvrd.

Allspice.latsrf — Function
latsrf([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, lonlat) -> Vector{SVector{3,Float64}}

Return the surface points of target (km, in the body-fixed frame fixref) at the planetocentric longitudes and latitudes lonlat, as latsrf_c.

lonlat holds the pairs (longitude, latitude) in radians: a 2 x n matrix, or a vector of n pairs. target and fixref are names or IDs; the frame must be centered on the target. method selects the surface:

  • "ELLIPSOID": the points are on the ellipsoid of the radii BODY<id>_RADII of the kernel pool. et is not used.
  • "DSK/UNPRIORITIZED[/SURFACES = <list>]": the points are on the loaded DSK segments at the epoch et (seconds past J2000 TDB). A ray starts outside of the body in the given direction and goes to the center; the point is its first intercept. The list names surfaces by name or by ID; without it every surface of the body is used.

A DSK method uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError:

  • :emptystring if method, target or fixref is an empty string;
  • :idcodenotfound if the target or a surface of the list is not known;
  • :noframe if the frame is not known, :invalidframe if it is not centered on the target;
  • :badmethodsyntax if method is not valid, :badpriorityspec if a DSK method does not have UNPRIORITIZED, :invalidmethod if method has a sub-observer point type;
  • :kernelvarnotfound if the radii are not loaded, :badaxislength if they are not three positive values (ellipsoid);
  • :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded, :dskdatanotfound if no DSK segment of the target with a listed surface is loaded, :pointnotfound if a ray meets no surface, :shapenotsupported if an intercept is on the far side of the center (DSK).

See also srfnrm, dskxsi.

Allspice.srfnrm — Function
srfnrm([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, srfpts) -> Vector{SVector{3,Float64}}

Return the outward unit normals of target at the surface points srfpts, in the body-fixed frame fixref, as srfnrm_c.

srfpts holds the points (km, in the frame fixref): a 3 x n matrix, or a vector of n 3-vectors. method, target, et and fixref are as for latsrf. For a DSK method the normal is that of the plate (the triangle of the DSK surface) on which the point lies.

Throws SpiceError:

  • the kinds of latsrf for method, target and fixref;
  • :kernelvarnotfound if the radii are not loaded, :badaxislength if they are not three positive values, :pointnotonsurface if a point is not on the ellipsoid within the point-membership margin PTMEMM, 1e-7 unless dskstl set another (ellipsoid);
  • :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded, :nodsksegments if no DSK segment of the target is loaded, :pointnotinsegment if no segment holds a point, :pointoffsurface if a point is not on a plate (DSK).

Illumination

Allspice.ilumin — Function
ilumin([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr, spoint)
    -> (trgepc, srfvec, phase, incdnc, emissn)

Return the illumination angles at the surface point spoint of target, with the Sun as the illumination source, as ilumin_c of CSPICE.

phase, incdnc and emissn are the phase angle, the solar incidence angle and the emission angle (rad). trgepc is the epoch at the point (seconds past J2000 TDB) and srfvec the vector from the observer to the point (km, body-fixed frame fixref). This is illumg with ilusrc = 10; see there for the arguments, the notes and the errors.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of Mars, and a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
spoint, = subpnt("NEAR POINT/ELLIPSOID", "MARS", et, "IAU_MARS", "LT+S", "EARTH")
trgepc, srfvec, phase, incdnc, emissn = ilumin("ELLIPSOID", "MARS", et, "IAU_MARS", "LT+S", "EARTH", spoint)
daylight = incdnc <= halfpi()          # the Sun is above the horizon of the point

See also illumg, illumf, phaseq, subslr.

Allspice.illumg — Function
illumg([ctx,] method, target, ilusrc, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr, spoint)
    -> (trgepc, srfvec, phase, incdnc, emissn)

Return the illumination angles at the surface point spoint of target, for the illumination source ilusrc and the observer obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as illumg_c of CSPICE.

  • trgepc: the epoch at the point (seconds past J2000 TDB). With a light time correction it is et minus (reception) or plus (transmission) the light time between the observer and the point. Without one it is et.
  • srfvec: the vector from the observer to the point, in km, in the body-fixed frame fixref at trgepc.
  • phase: the angle at the point between the directions to the source and to the observer (rad).
  • incdnc: the angle between the outward surface normal and the direction to the source (rad).
  • emissn: the angle between the outward surface normal and the direction to the observer (rad).

spoint is in km in fixref (3 elements). The call does not check that it is on the surface. method selects the surface normal: "ELLIPSOID" or "DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"; the DSK form can end with "/SURFACES = <list>". abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. With a correction, the source is taken where it appears from the point at trgepc.

With "ELLIPSOID", et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual, and the call does not allocate when the bodies and the frame are integer IDs. A DSK method uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :badmethodsyntax if method is malformed, :badpriorityspec if a DSK method does not have UNPRIORITIZED, :invalidmethod if it holds a sub-point type;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body; ilusrc can be either of them (ilusrc == target puts the source at the target center, so incdnc > π/2 on the ellipsoid);
  • :noframe if fixref is not known, :invalidframe if it is not centered on target;
  • :badarg if spoint does not have 3 elements;
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of target are missing or not valid (ellipsoid).

Also throws the errors of spkcpt and, with a DSK method, of srfnrm.

See also ilumin, illumf, gfilum.

Allspice.illumf — Function
illumf([ctx,] method, target, ilusrc, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr, spoint)
    -> (trgepc, srfvec, phase, incdnc, emissn, visibl, lit)

Return the outputs of illumg and two flags, as illumf_c of CSPICE: visibl tells whether the observer sees the point, and lit whether the source lights it.

With "ELLIPSOID", visibl is emissn <= π/2 and lit is incdnc <= π/2. With a DSK method, a flag is also false if the surface blocks the ray from the point toward the observer or toward the source.

With a DSK method, the angles and the flags use all loaded surfaces of the target: the SURFACES list of method is ignored. The DSK segments are those that cover trgepc; illumg takes those that cover et. Both are as in CSPICE.

The arguments, the notes on et, allocation and tolerances, and the errors are those of illumg, except :dskdatanotfound if DSK files are loaded but none has data for target.

See also ilumin, illumg.

Allspice.phaseq — Function
phaseq([ctx,] et, target, illmn, obsrvr, abcorr) -> phase

Return the phase angle at target at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), in radians in [0, π], as phaseq_c of CSPICE. It is the angle between the directions from the target to the source illmn and to the observer obsrvr.

With a correction, the target is taken as seen by the observer, and the source as seen by the target at et - lt, where lt is the light time between the observer and the target. Bodies are names or integer IDs. et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual. The call does not allocate when the bodies are integer IDs.

Throws SpiceError(:invalidoption) if abcorr is not a valid correction or is a transmission correction, SpiceError(:bodiesnotdistinct) if two of the three bodies are the same, and SpiceError(:idcodenotfound) if a body name is not known. Also throws the errors of spkpos.

See also ilumin, gfpa.

Allspice.trgsep — Function
trgsep([ctx,] et, targ1, shape1, frame1, targ2, shape2, frame2, obsrvr, abcorr) -> angle

Return the angular separation (rad) of two targets as seen by the observer obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as trgsep_c of CSPICE.

Each target is a point (shape = "POINT") or a sphere ("SPHERE") whose radius is the largest of the three radii of the body in the kernel pool. For spheres the angle is the separation of the limbs: the angle between the centers minus the two angular radii, so it is negative when the disks overlap. The frames frame1 and frame2 are not used by these shapes, as in CSPICE. Bodies are names or integer IDs. abcorr takes the values of spkezr, and each position is corrected as seen by the observer. et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual.

Throws SpiceError:

  • :emptystring if a string argument is empty;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if two of the three bodies are the same;
  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :notrecognized if a shape is not POINT or SPHERE;
  • :kernelvarnotfound, :typemismatch, :invalidcount or :badaxislength if the radii of a sphere are missing or not valid;
  • :insidebody if the observer is not outside a sphere;
  • :badarg if et is not finite;
  • the errors of spkpos.

Examples

# Needs an SPK with the Sun, the Earth and the Moon, and a PCK with their radii.
et = str2et("2024 APR 08 18:18:00 UTC")
sep = trgsep(et, "MOON", "SPHERE", " ", "SUN", "SPHERE", " ", "EARTH", "LT+S")
overlap = sep < 0           # the disks of the Moon and of the Sun overlap

See also phaseq, gfsep.

Occultation, limb and terminator

Allspice.occult — Function
occult([ctx,] targ1, shape1, frame1, targ2, shape2, frame2, abcorr, obsrvr, et) -> Int

Return the occultation state of two targets as seen by obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as occult_c of CSPICE:

CodeMeaning
-3total occultation of the first target by the second
-2annular occultation of the first target by the second
-1partial occultation of the first target by the second
0no occultation
1partial occultation of the second target by the first
2annular occultation of the second target by the first
3total occultation of the second target by the first

shape1 and shape2 are "ELLIPSOID", "POINT" or "DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"; the DSK form can end with "/SURFACES = <list>". At least one target must be extended, and a DSK target needs a point as the other target. With a point and an extended target, the only codes are 0, ±2 (the point is in front) and ±3 (the point is behind).

frame1 and frame2 are the body-fixed frames of the extended targets, each centered on its target. The frame of a point target is not used; pass " ".

abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. Stellar aberration is not applied: "LT+S" acts as "LT", as in CSPICE. et cannot be a ForwardDiff.Dual, because the result is discrete. The call does not allocate when the bodies and the frames are integer IDs and the shapes are ellipsoids or points. A DSK shape uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :badarg if et is a ForwardDiff.Dual;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if two of the three bodies are the same;
  • :invalidshape if a shape is not one of the three values, :badmethodsyntax if a DSK shape string is malformed, :badpriorityspec if it does not have UNPRIORITIZED;
  • :invalidshapecombo if both targets are points, or if a DSK target is paired with an extended target;
  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :invalidframe if the frame of an extended target is blank, not known or not centered on the target;
  • :notdisjoint if the observer is inside a target, or if the targets overlap;
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of an ellipsoid target are missing or not valid, :noloadeddskfiles if a target is a DSK shape and no DSK file is loaded, :dskdatanotfound if a DSK target has no data loaded.

Also throws the errors of spkpos.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of the Moon and the Sun, and a
# leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2024 APR 08 18:18:00 UTC")
code = occult("MOON", "ELLIPSOID", "IAU_MOON", "SUN", "ELLIPSOID", "IAU_SUN", "LT", "EARTH", et)
code > 0        # true if the Moon hides a part of the Sun as seen from the Earth center
code == 3       # true if the eclipse is total there

See also gfoclt, fovtrg.

Allspice.limbpt — Function
limbpt([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, corloc, obsrvr, refvec, rolstp,
       ncuts, schstp, soltol, maxn) -> (npts, points, epochs, tangts)

Return limb points of target as seen by obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as limbpt_c of CSPICE.

The points lie in ncuts half planes bounded by the line from the target center to the observer. Half plane i contains refvec rotated by (i - 1) * rolstp radians, counterclockwise about the vector from the target center to the observer. refvec has 3 elements, in the body-fixed frame fixref.

  • npts: npts[i] is the number of points found in half plane i (ncuts elements).
  • points: the limb points, in km in fixref, half plane after half plane.
  • epochs: the epoch at each point, in seconds past J2000 TDB (see corloc).
  • tangts: the vector from the observer to each point, in km in fixref at that epoch.

The three lists have sum(npts) elements, not maxn as the arrays of CSPICE. The points of half plane i start at index sum(npts[1:i-1]) + 1. points and tangts are of type Vector{SVector{3,Float64}}, and epochs is a Vector{Float64}.

methodLimb points
"TANGENT/ELLIPSOID"points of the reference ellipsoid where a ray from the observer is tangent; one per half plane
"TANGENT/DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"points of the DSK surface where a ray from the observer is tangent; none, one or several per half plane
"GUIDED/ELLIPSOID"as "TANGENT/ELLIPSOID"
"GUIDED/DSK/UNPRIORITIZED"the outermost DSK surface point on the ray from the center of the ellipsoid limb through each ellipsoid limb point; at most one per half plane

A DSK method can end with "/SURFACES = <list>".

corloc is the place for which the light time and the stellar aberration are computed: "CENTER" (the target center; all points have the same epoch) or "ELLIPSOID LIMB" (each limb point of the reference ellipsoid; TANGENT methods only).

schstp and soltol (radians) are used by "TANGENT/DSK" only. The ray is rotated in the half plane in steps of schstp; a tangent point is missed if the ray enters and leaves the surface within one step. Each tangent ray is then located to within the angle soltol. With an ellipsoid method both values are ignored.

maxn is the largest total number of points. abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. et cannot be a ForwardDiff.Dual. A DSK method uses the tolerances that dskstl set on the context.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :badarg if et is a ForwardDiff.Dual;
  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body;
  • :noframe if fixref is not known, :invalidframe if it is not centered on target;
  • :badmethodsyntax if method is malformed, :badpriorityspec if a DSK method does not have UNPRIORITIZED, :invalidlimbtype if it has neither TANGENT nor GUIDED, :invalidmethod if it holds a sub-point or a terminator type;
  • :invalidlocus if corloc is not one of the two values, :badlimblocusmix for GUIDED with "ELLIPSOID LIMB";
  • :zerovector if refvec is zero, :degeneratecase if it is parallel to the line from the target to the observer;
  • :invalidsize if maxn < 1, :invalidcount unless 1 <= ncuts <= maxn, :invalidrollstep if rolstp is zero and ncuts > 1;
  • with a DSK method, :invalidsearchstep if schstp is not positive, :invalidtolerance if soltol is not positive, and, with TANGENT, :invalidconststep if schstp is too small to change the angle;
  • :outofroom if more than maxn points are found, :arraytoosmall if one half plane has more than 2000;
  • :noseparation if the observer is at the target center;
  • :kernelvarnotfound if the radii of target are not loaded, :badradiuscount if there are not 3 of them, :invalidaxislength if one is not positive;
  • :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded, :dskdatanotfound if no DSK data are loaded for target (DSK method).

Also throws the errors of spkpos.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of Mars, and a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
z = [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]                     # north pole of Mars in IAU_MARS
npts, points, epochs, tangts = limbpt("TANGENT/ELLIPSOID", "MARS", et, "IAU_MARS", "LT+S",
    "CENTER", "EARTH", z, twopi() / 36, 36, 0.0, 0.0, 36)
length(points) == sum(npts) == 36       # one point per half plane on an ellipsoid
lonlat = [reclat(p)[2:3] for p in points]

See also termpt, tangpt, edlimb.

Allspice.termpt — Function
termpt([ctx,] method, ilusrc, target, et, fixref, abcorr, corloc, obsrvr, refvec,
       rolstp, ncuts, schstp, soltol, maxn) -> (npts, points, epochs, trmvcs)

Return terminator points of target lit by the source ilusrc, as seen by obsrvr at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as termpt_c of CSPICE.

The points lie in ncuts half planes bounded by the line from the target center to the source center. Half plane i contains refvec (body-fixed frame fixref) rotated by (i - 1) * rolstp radians, counterclockwise about the vector from the target center to the source. The outputs have the layout of limbpt, and are empty for ncuts = 0; trmvcs holds the vectors from the observer to the points (km).

The source is a sphere with the largest radius of ilusrc. method joins the shadow, the point definition and the shape, for example "UMBRAL/TANGENT/ELLIPSOID" or "PENUMBRAL/TANGENT/DSK/UNPRIORITIZED":

  • UMBRAL: the boundary of the region from which no part of the source is visible. PENUMBRAL: the boundary of the region from which the whole source is visible.
  • TANGENT: points where a ray tangent to the source is tangent to the surface. GUIDED: on a DSK surface, the outermost point on the ray from the target center through each terminator point of the reference ellipsoid.
  • ELLIPSOID, or DSK/UNPRIORITIZED with an optional "/SURFACES = <list>".

corloc is "CENTER" or "ELLIPSOID TERMINATOR" (TANGENT methods only), with the meaning it has in limbpt. abcorr is "NONE" or a reception correction. schstp, soltol, maxn, et and the dskstl tolerances are as in limbpt.

As in CSPICE, a half plane with n points takes n^2 of the maxn places, not n. With an ellipsoid n is 1. With a DSK method, :outofroom can occur before maxn points are found, so give maxn a margin.

Throws the kinds of limbpt for et, the bodies, the frame, refvec, rolstp and maxn < 1, and SpiceError with the kind

  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not valid or is a transmission correction;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target is also the observer or the source;
  • :invalidtermtype if method has neither TANGENT nor GUIDED, :invalidshadow if it has neither UMBRAL nor PENUMBRAL, :invalidmethod if it holds a sub-point type;
  • :invalidlocus if corloc is not one of the two values, :badtermlocusmix for GUIDED with "ELLIPSOID TERMINATOR";
  • :invalidcount unless 0 <= ncuts <= maxn;
  • :objectstooclose if the source touches the bounding sphere of the target, the sphere about its center that contains it;
  • in a TANGENT/DSK search, :invalidconststep if schstp is not positive and :invalidtolerance if soltol is not positive;
  • :noconvergence if the search for one terminator point of the ellipsoid takes more than 20 iterations;
  • :kernelvarnotfound if the radii of target or ilusrc are not loaded, :badradiuscount if there are not 3 of them, :invalidaxislength if one is not positive;
  • :noloadeddskfiles if no DSK file is loaded, :dskdatanotfound if no DSK data are loaded for target (DSK method).

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii of Mars and the Sun and the rotation of Mars, and
# a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
z = [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]
npts, points, epochs, trmvcs = termpt("UMBRAL/TANGENT/ELLIPSOID", "SUN", "MARS", et,
    "IAU_MARS", "LT+S", "CENTER", "EARTH", z, twopi() / 36, 36, 0.0, 0.0, 36)
lonlat = [reclat(p)[2:3] for p in points]

See also limbpt, edterm, ilumin.

Allspice.edterm — Function
edterm([ctx,] trmtyp, source, target, et, fixref, abcorr, obsrvr, npts)
    -> (trgepc, obspos::SVector{3}, trmpts::Vector{SVector{3,Float64}})

Return npts points of the terminator of an ellipsoidal body that a spherical body lights, as edterm_c of CSPICE.

trmtyp is "UMBRAL" or "PENUMBRAL". source is the illumination source and target the lit body. et is the epoch at the observer obsrvr, in seconds past J2000 TDB. fixref is a body-fixed frame of the target. abcorr is an aberration correction of spkezr, for example "NONE" or "LT+S". Bodies and frames are names or IDs.

  • trgepc: the epoch at the target, in seconds past J2000 TDB: et corrected for light time if abcorr asks for it.
  • obspos: the position of the observer relative to the target center, in km, in fixref at trgepc.
  • trmpts: the terminator points relative to the target center, in km, in fixref at trgepc.

The call needs SPK data for the three bodies, the orientation of fixref, and the BODY<id>_RADII of the source and the target.

Throws:

  • SpiceError(:notranslation) if a body or frame name is not known.
  • SpiceError(:invalidfixref) if fixref does not have its center at the target.
  • SpiceError(:spkinvalidoption) if abcorr is not known.
  • SpiceError(:notsupported) for another terminator type.
  • SpiceError(:invalidsize) if npts < 1.
  • SpiceError(:objectstooclose) if the source touches the target.
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of the source or the target are missing or not valid.

Allspice.tangpt — Function
tangpt([ctx,] method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, corloc, obsrvr, dref, dvec)
    -> (tanpt, alt, range, srfpt, trgepc, srfvec)

Return the tangent point of the ray from obsrvr along dvec on the reference ellipsoid of target, as tangpt_c of CSPICE.

  • tanpt: the point of the ray nearest to the ellipsoid, in km in the body-fixed frame fixref.
  • alt: the altitude of tanpt above the ellipsoid (km).
  • range: the distance from the observer to tanpt (km).
  • srfpt: the point of the ellipsoid nearest to tanpt, in km in fixref.
  • trgepc: the epoch at the point named by corloc (seconds past J2000 TDB). Without a correction it is et.
  • srfvec: the vector from the observer to srfpt, in km in fixref at trgepc.

If the ray meets the ellipsoid, alt is 0 and tanpt equals srfpt. If the ray points away from the target, tanpt is the observer position and range is 0.

dvec is the ray direction (3 elements, not zero) in the frame dref. method must be "ELLIPSOID". corloc is "TANGENT POINT" or "SURFACE POINT": the point for which the light time and the stellar aberration are computed. abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. et is in seconds past J2000 TDB and cannot be a ForwardDiff.Dual. The call does not allocate when the bodies and the frames are integer IDs.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :badarg if et is a ForwardDiff.Dual;
  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :notsupported if method is not "ELLIPSOID" or corloc is not one of the two values;
  • :zerovector if dvec is zero, :badarg if it does not have 3 elements;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body;
  • :kernelvarnotfound if the radii of target are not loaded, :invalidcount if there are not 3 of them, :invalidradii if one is not positive;
  • :noframe if fixref or dref is not known, :invalidframe if fixref is not centered on target;
  • :noseparation if the observer is at the target center, :invalidgeometry if it is inside or on the ellipsoid.

Also throws the errors of spkpos.

See also limbpt, sincpt, npedln.

Allspice.fovtrg — Function
fovtrg([ctx,] inst, target, tshape, tframe, abcorr, obsrvr, et) -> Bool

Return true if the body target is in the field of view of the instrument inst at et (seconds past J2000 TDB), as seen by obsrvr, as fovtrg_c of CSPICE.

tshape is the model of the target:

tshapeTesttframe
"ELLIPSOID"any part of the reference ellipsoid is in the field of viewbody-fixed frame of the target
"POINT"the target center is in the field of viewnot used; pass " "

inst is an instrument name or ID with a field of view in the kernel pool (see getfov). obsrvr is the body that carries the instrument. The instrument frame is evaluated at et, so a CK and an SCLK kernel for the spacecraft are needed if that frame is a CK frame. abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. et cannot be a ForwardDiff.Dual, because the result is discrete.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :badarg if et is a ForwardDiff.Dual;
  • :invalidoption if tshape is "RAY" (use fovray) or if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body or instrument name is not known;
  • :invalidshape if tshape is not "ELLIPSOID" or "POINT";
  • :bodiesnotdistinct if target and obsrvr are the same body;
  • for an ellipsoid target, :invalidframe if tframe is blank, not known, or not centered on target, and :frameinfonotfound if it has no definition loaded;
  • :unknownframe if the instrument frame is not known;
  • :fovtoowide if the angular radius of the field of view about its axis is not below 90 degrees minus 1e-6 rad;
  • for a rectangle or a polygon: :degeneratecase if two neighbor boundary vectors are parallel, :facenotfound if the boundary vectors are not in one half space, :notsupported if a boundary vector is within 2e-12 rad of the plane of a side of the field of view, :invalidfov if a vector is 90 degrees or more from the axis;
  • for a circle or an ellipse: :degeneratecase if a semi-axis is zero;
  • :notdisjoint if the observer is inside the target ellipsoid.

Also throws the errors of getfov and spkpos, and the kinds of srfrec if the radii of an ellipsoid target are missing or not valid.

Examples

Allspice.getfov — Function
getfov([ctx,] instid, room=10000) -> (shape, frame, bsight, bounds)

Return the field of view of the instrument with the integer ID instid, as getfov_c of CSPICE.

  • shape: "CIRCLE", "ELLIPSE", "RECTANGLE" or "POLYGON".
  • frame: the name of the frame of the vectors.
  • bsight: the boresight vector, an SVector{3}.
  • bounds: a new Vector of boundary vectors (SVector{3}): 1 for a circle, 2 for an ellipse, 4 for a rectangle, the corners for a polygon.

The vectors are not unit vectors in general; only their directions matter. CSPICE also returns the number of boundary vectors; here it is length(bounds).

The data come from the pool variables INS<instid>_FOV_FRAME, INS<instid>_FOV_SHAPE, INS<instid>_BORESIGHT and the other INS<instid>_FOV_* variables of an instrument kernel. INS<instid>_FOV_CLASS_SPEC selects the form: CORNERS (the default) gives the boundary vectors, ANGLES gives angles from the boresight. room is the largest number of boundary vectors accepted.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :framemissing, :shapemissing, :boresightmissing or :boundarymissing if the frame, the shape, the boresight or the boundary variable is not in the kernel pool;
  • :shapenotsupported if the shape is not one of the four, or is a polygon given by angles; :unsupportedspec if the specification is not CORNERS or ANGLES;
  • :badboresightspec if the boresight does not hold 3 numbers, :zeroboresight if it is zero;
  • :badboundary if the boundary does not hold a multiple of 3 numbers, if the number of vectors does not fit the shape, or if a half angle of a rectangle is 90 degrees or more;
  • :boundarytoobig if there are more than room boundary vectors;
  • :refvectormissing, :refanglemissing, :crossanglemissing or :unitsmissing if a variable of the ANGLES form is absent, :badrefvectorspec if the reference vector does not hold 3 numbers or is parallel to the boresight.

Also throws the errors of convrt for the angle unit.

See also getfvn, fovtrg, fovray.

Azimuth and elevation

Allspice.azlcpo — Function
azlcpo([ctx,] method, target, et, abcorr, azccw, elplsz, obspos, obsctr, obsref)
    -> (azlsta, lt)

Return the range, azimuth and elevation of target and their rates, seen from an observer at a constant position on the body obsctr, and the one-way light time lt (s), as azlcpo_c of CSPICE.

azlsta is an SVector{6}: range (km), azimuth and elevation (rad), then their rates (km/s, rad/s). obspos is the observer position relative to the center of obsctr (km, 3 elements), in the body-fixed frame obsref, which must be centered on obsctr. et is in seconds past J2000 TDB.

The angles are measured in the topocentric frame at the observer. Its +Z axis is the outward normal of the reference ellipsoid of obsctr at the point nearest to the observer, and its +X axis points north. For an observer on the polar axis, +X is the +X axis of obsref. azccw and elplsz are the flags of azlrec.

method must be "ELLIPSOID". abcorr is a correction string of spkezr or an Aberration. et can be a ForwardDiff.Dual. The call does not allocate when the bodies and the frame are integer IDs.

Throws SpiceError with the kind

  • :invalidmethod if method is not "ELLIPSOID";
  • :invalidoption if abcorr is not a valid correction;
  • :idcodenotfound if a body name is not known;
  • :unknownframe if obsref is not known, :invalidframe if it is not centered on obsctr;
  • :badarg if obspos does not have 3 elements;
  • :pointonzaxis if the target is on the local vertical;
  • the kinds of srfrec if the radii of obsctr are missing or not valid.

Also throws the errors of spkcpo.

Examples

# Needs an SPK, a PCK with the radii and the rotation of the Earth, and a leap seconds kernel.
et = str2et("2025 MAR 01 12:00:00 UTC")
station = georec(-116.79 * rpd(), 35.25 * rpd(), 1.0, 6378.1366, 1 / 298.257)   # km
azlsta, lt = azlcpo("ELLIPSOID", "MOON", et, "LT+S", false, true, station, "EARTH", "IAU_EARTH")
az, el = azlsta[2] * dpr(), azlsta[3] * dpr()    # degrees; azimuth clockwise from north

See also spkcpo, recazl, dazldr.
